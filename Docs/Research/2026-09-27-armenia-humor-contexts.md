## Scholarship and linguistic limits

Haykanush Sharuryan's 2024 article in *Հայագիտական հանդես* examines the English translation of Hakob Paronyan's *Harms of Politeness*, including omissions, dialogue changes and difficult forms of address. Its useful implication for this project is that comic meaning depends on linguistic choices and context; literal translation alone is insufficient. The article is not a licence to copy Paronyan's jokes or a survey proving present-day national preferences. Paronyan's Western Armenian literary context must not be mislabeled as modern Eastern Armenian. [Article, pp. 124–140](https://arar.sci.am/Content/381806/124-140.pdf).

The production language is Eastern Armenian, with Armenian script and conventional punctuation. Wordplay must operate in Armenian: polysemy, idiomatic literalisation, register contrast, letter shapes and the difference between formal written language and a concrete situation. Arbitrary substitutions of English homophones are unsuitable. A joke set in Gyumri is not automatically written in Gyumri dialect; this expansion does not imitate regional accents or claim dialect coverage.

The setting itself should help cause the comic turn. Merely attaching “in Yerevan” to a generic imported premise is insufficient. Everyday office, school, technology and civic scenes are legitimate contemporary settings, but the text should contain a meaningful local practice, language feature, named place, material craft or cultural activity.

## Editorial and coverage rules

Each new record has one primary category and one stable ID. Exactly 177 new wholesome and office-proper records and 178 in each other category are required. Existing 200 records must not be changed, silently relabeled or counted twice. Batch validation checks schema, stable IDs, Unicode-normalised text uniqueness and exact category totals against the existing corpus. Exact-text checks do not establish premise novelty or human-level editorial quality.

Wholesome and child-safe scenes use cooperative misunderstandings and playful discoveries. Dad jokes prefer concise literal readings. Nerd jokes use accurate reasoning, computing or scientific ideas in a researched local setting. Surreal jokes clearly invent impossible events. Dark humour concerns time, obsolete objects, deadlines and mildly existential frustrations; edgy material criticises fictional processes and overconfident technology. Neither category uses atrocity, war, violence, sexual content, national superiority or neighbouring peoples as punchlines.

All new material is AI-authored original draft content. Institutional sources establish the settings described here; they do not endorse the jokes. Notes must identify the actual context and mechanism and state that native-speaker review has not occurred. Neighbour-country rivalry is outside this Armenia batch until a specific relational context is researched and represented; no nationality is a shortcut for a personality defect.
