# French corpus: cultural contexts and editorial choices

Research and authorship date: 27 September 2026. These are original AI-authored French texts, not collected traditional jokes, translations, or native-speaker-approved material. Country and culture labels describe an editorial setting, not the identity or temperament of an author or reader. Existing `fr.json` records are preserved; the new category files add concrete French references.

## Research basis

The Académie française describes the circulation of *humeur/humour* between French and English and humor’s attention to incongruity. This supports understated reversals and absurd situations, without endorsing national personality claims. Its language notes provide a check on accents, expressions and lexical ambiguity. Wordplay must work in French itself rather than rely on English meanings. Sources: [Humeur, humour](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/humeur-humour), [Questions de langue](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/questions-de-langue).

The baguette is both an artisanal process and a social practice, making bakery queues, crust, crumbs and repeated neighborhood encounters specific, useful scenes. The celebratory gastronomic meal involves a sequence and shared conversation; it should not be mistaken for every French weekday dinner. Sources: [UNESCO baguette inscription](https://ich.unesco.org/fr/RL/les-savoir-faire-artisanaux-et-la-culture-de-la-baguette-de-pain-01883), [Atout France: gastronomic meal](https://www.france.fr/fr/article/repas-gastronomique-francais/).

Brittany offers much more than weather shorthand: collective fest-noz dancing, learning by observation, galettes of buckwheat, galette-saucisse and kouign-amann provide distinct physical and culinary situations. UNESCO describes a living, evolving practice, not a frozen folk costume. Sources: [UNESCO fest-noz](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/fest-noz-festive-gathering-based-on-the-collective-practice-of-traditional-dances-of-brittany-00707), [Atout France: Brittany](https://www.france.fr/fr/article/bretagne/).

Regional settings are deliberately varied. Lyon supplies traboules, bouchons, the Croix-Rousse slopes and the Fête des Lumières. Alsace supplies colombage houses, seasonal markets and named pastries. Nice supplies socca, Cours Saleya and the Promenade des Anglais. These references generate different situations, rather than substituting city names into one joke. Sources: [Lyon](https://www.france.fr/fr/destination/lyon/), [Alsace and Lorraine](https://www.france.fr/fr/destination/alsace-lorraine/), [Alsace tourism market calendar](https://www.noel.alsace/marches-de-noel/calendrier-des-marches-de-noel/), [Nice](https://www.france.fr/fr/destination/nice/).

The Fête de la musique is a useful contemporary shared context: June 21, amateur and professional participation, many styles and places beyond Paris. Institutional discussion of burlesque supports benign physical mishaps, while the BnF’s treatment of satirical drawing suggests institutions and pretension as targets. No joke, distinctive passage, named comedian’s style or punchline has been taken from these sources. Sources: [Ministère de la Culture: Fête de la musique](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/evenements-nationaux/fete-de-la-musique), [Histoire des arts: burlesque](https://histoiredesarts.culture.gouv.fr/Toutes-les-ressources/Ciclic/Histoire-du-cinema-burlesque), [BnF: GéBé](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/agenda/avec-gebe-regards-croises-sur-un-dessinateur-hors-norme).

## Authoring rules and limitations

New records use a recognizable French cultural, linguistic or local reference in their actual text. Contemporary school, transport and office scenes can use stable named objects such as a cahier Seyès, Navigo, TGV, CERFA or RTT; invented incidents are not claims about official rules, timetables or service reliability. Food and dance references remain affectionate; bureaucracy and objects bear the sharper satire. Child-safe records exclude alcohol as the joke’s reward. Dark and edgy records remain mild, without graphic harm, sexuality or attacks on protected groups.

French-language use extends well beyond France. This collection does not claim to represent all Francophone people, overseas cultures, or every region of France. Regional words are contextualized rather than presented as exotic traits. Dialects and accents are not mocked. Native editorial review remains outstanding, especially for regional naturalness, whether wordplay lands aloud, category fit and unintended repetition across a large corpus.

Target additions: 177 wholesome, 177 office-proper, and 178 each dad, kid-safe, nerd, surreal, dark, edgy and puns. With the original 200 records this yields 200 records in each of nine primary categories. IDs use `intl-v2-fr-{category}-{number}`. Literal original texts are assembled into JSON; topic metadata does not generate or substitute joke content. Automated checks establish schema and exact-duplicate validity, not artistic quality or native approval.
