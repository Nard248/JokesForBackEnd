# Norway and Nordic neighbour contexts

Research date: 2026-09-27. Authoring language: Norwegian Bokmål (`nb`). Country setting: Norway (`NO`). Sweden (`SE`) and Finland (`FI`) are secondary country contexts on individual jokes. These records are new AI-authored comedy, not quotations from traditional joke collections and not native-reviewed work.

## Language and scope

Bokmål and Nynorsk are distinct written standards. A country selector must not silently choose a language, and Bokmål content must not be advertised as Nynorsk, Swedish, Finnish or Sámi. Dialects are not interchangeable with written standards. The initial collection uses readable Bokmål, retaining local vocabulary where it is the joke's mechanism. A future `nn` collection needs separate authoring and review. [Språkrådet: normering](https://sprakradet.no/nyord-og-rettskrivingsendringer/normering-av-norsk/); [NorDial research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04989).

The user named Norway as a main country and Sweden/Finland as examples of neighbour humour. The collection therefore includes Norwegian-language scenes involving those neighbours; it does not claim 1,800 Swedish-language or Finnish-language jokes. Secondary country tags make those scenes discoverable without manufacturing translated copies or adding quota credit.

These sources substantiate settings, not a claim that every resident shares a particular personality. Tourism sources are selective presentations; the story premises are editorial inventions. No factual inference about intelligence, honesty or temperament follows from nationality.

## Authoring decisions

Neighbour banter works best as a small scene with named roles or friends, competing habits and a reversal. The Norwegian narrator can lose the argument. Sweden is not a stock punchline for stupidity, and Finnish characters are not reduced to silence, drinking or danger. The sauna jokes concern planning, technology and perspective, not unsafe endurance contests. Sporting boasting is a fictional character's behaviour, not a factual statement about current results.

Norwegian comic mechanisms used here include understatement, literal readings of compound words, overly confident outdoor planning, the mismatch between elaborate equipment and simple pleasures, and bureaucratic language transferred into domestic life. These are authoring choices, not a scientific ranking of Norwegian humour. Wordplay is written directly in Bokmål: an English pun is not translated unless both senses actually survive.

The nine primary categories retain their app meaning. Wholesome has a warm social resolution; office-proper concerns recognisable workplace absurdity; dad uses deliberately groan-inducing literalism; kid-safe uses clear play and imagination; nerd requires a technical or scientific mechanism; surreal breaks reality; dark uses non-graphic mortality or existential irony; edgy points at status, pretension and institutional absurdity; puns depends on the Norwegian wording. Secondary themes support food, travel, weather, family and other browsing.

Each literal authored record receives one primary category. A Swedish or Finnish setting receives a secondary country tag only when the text supports it. Every record remains in the Norway/Bokmål main collection, so a joke cannot satisfy multiple country-collection quotas merely through extra tags.

## Review standard

The machine checks stable identifiers, Unicode-normalised duplicates, valid categories/themes and exact authored/installed category counts. They cannot certify funniness, dialect authenticity or native editorial quality. Content keeps `editorial_status=generated`; human review can later change that status individually. The import preserves removals, age/tier decisions and existing editorial edits.
